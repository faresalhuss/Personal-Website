# Brand & Growth Strategy — fareshusseini.com

**Prepared for:** Fa'res Husseini
**Date:** May 2026
**Lens:** A brand/growth advisor's read of the site and your situation — solo
operator, phone + laptop, no content team, no paid-media budget, trying to make
the biggest possible dent.

This is written to be direct. The site is genuinely well-built; the honest risk
is that it's a beautiful house on a street nobody walks down yet. The work ahead
is less about the website and more about the engine that points people at it.

---

## TL;DR — the five things that matter

1. **Your newsletter is silently losing emails right now.** In production the form
   saves signups to a temporary folder that gets wiped. Connect a real (free)
   provider before you drive a single visitor to it. _(Part 6 — this is the one
   urgent fix.)_
2. **The site is ahead of your content.** It promises a weekly email and essays
   that don't exist yet. Either start producing, or soften the promises until you
   do. Promising a cadence you're not keeping quietly erodes trust.
3. **Your positioning is one notch too generic.** "Entrepreneur, creator, writer
   who builds small businesses" describes ten thousand people. You have a sharper,
   true wedge — _build toward law school in public_ — use it. (Part 1.)
4. **The site is the home base, not the megaphone.** Growth happens on TikTok / IG
   / YouTube; the site converts that attention into something you own (email).
   Get the hub-and-spoke loop right. (Parts 4 & 5.)
5. **You can run all of this from a phone for $0.** The constraint is real but not
   limiting — the plan below is built around it.

## Part 1 — Positioning (the foundation everything else hangs on)

### What the site says today
- Hero: _"I build small businesses, and I'm having fun along the way."_
- Intro: _"…someone documenting what it actually takes to grow a business from zero."_
- Identity tags: _Entrepreneur · Creator · Writer._

This is a solid "build-in-public founder" lane. It's honest and on-trend. The
problem is that it's **crowded and undifferentiated** — the words could belong to
any of thousands of founder accounts.

### The wedge you're not using
Your real story has three things most build-in-public founders don't:

1. **A countdown.** You're building businesses _while preparing for law school in
   2027._ That's a built-in narrative arc with tension and an end date — "can he
   build something that works before day one of law school?" People follow stories
   with stakes and a clock.
2. **An operator's edge.** You're not a generic founder — you run a marketing firm.
   You can teach distribution while doing it. That's rare and immediately useful.
3. **An outsider's path.** Saudi Arabia → California → Atlanta, economics degree,
   an interest in Islamic economics and the legal system. That's a distinct point
   of view, not a personality-free "hustle" account.

### Recommended one-liner (north star, not necessarily site copy)
> _"I'm building real businesses before I start law school in 2027 — and showing
> the honest version of what that actually takes."_

Keep the warm, learner's voice you already use. Just point it at a sharper target.
Everything downstream (content, newsletter, bio links) gets easier once the
sentence above is the filter for "is this on-brand or not."

## Part 3 — What CAN be improved (worthwhile, not urgent)

- **A lightweight "Now" page.** One page you update monthly: what you're building,
  what you're reading, what's next. Cheap to maintain, great for returning visitors
  and for AI/search ("what is Fa'res working on now").
- **Social proof, when you earn it.** A single real testimonial from a Clicks &
  Clients client, or a metric ("helped X firms book Y calls"), would do more for
  credibility than any design change. Add it the moment it's true.
- **First-party analytics clarity.** You already have Vercel Analytics. Define the
  _one_ number that matters (email signups), and watch it. Ignore vanity pageviews.
- **An "as featured / building" strip.** Once Animedic launches and the podcast is
  live, a simple logo/links row ties the brand together.
- **Repurpose the Books page as a magnet.** It's your most "content-rich" page
  already. A "my reading list" is a classic, evergreen, shareable asset — link to
  it from social and offer the full list to subscribers.
- **OG image polish per page.** You generate them dynamically; make sure the
  homepage and About share images look intentional, since those are what get
  posted when someone shares you.

## Part 4 — What goes on the SITE vs. on the PLATFORMS

The single most important mental model: **hub and spoke.**

- **The platforms (TikTok, IG, YouTube) are the spokes** — rented attention. Their
  job is reach: get discovered by strangers.
- **The site + email list is the hub** — owned audience. Its job is to convert that
  attention into a relationship you control and can't be de-platformed from.

You don't choose between them. The spokes feed the hub.

### What lives where

| Content | Primary home | Why |
|--------|--------------|-----|
| Short-form video (hooks, lessons, behind-the-scenes) | **TikTok / IG Reels / YT Shorts** | Discovery engines. This is your top-of-funnel. The algorithm shows you to strangers; the site never will. |
| Longer "building in public" videos / vlogs | **YouTube** | Search-durable, builds depth and trust, monetizable later. The podcast (_Wondering Out Loud_) lives here too. |
| Day-to-day moments, polls, story-driven updates | **Instagram (Stories/Reels)** | Relationship + reach with existing followers. |
| **The weekly note (your core thinking)** | **Email (owned) + mirrored on site** | The asset you own forever. Never make email-only people go _only_ to a platform. |
| **Essays / long-form writing** | **Your site (`/writing`)** | SEO + AI-discovery durable, demonstrates depth, you own the URL. Syndicate snippets out to social. |
| Your story / who you are | **Your site (`/about`)** | The destination every platform bio points to. |
| Reading list / recommendations | **Your site (`/books`)** | Evergreen, shareable, already built, affiliate-aligned. |
| "Start here" / what I'm doing now | **Your site** | The hub's job is orientation + conversion. |

**Rule of thumb:** _Create on platforms, capture on the site._ If a piece of
content is doing its job, it ends with "full version / weekly note at
fareshusseini.com."

## Part 5 — Growing fast in May 2026 without paid media

Organic growth in 2026 has one dominant truth: **short-form video is the cheapest
distribution that has ever existed, and it rewards consistency over production
value.** A founder talking honestly to a phone camera outperforms polished brand
content. That is perfect for your constraints.

### The core loop (repeat weekly)
```
1. Do the work (run C&C, build Animedic, prep for law school)
2. Capture one honest lesson from it (phone, 30–60s)
3. Post it as a Short/Reel/TikTok across all three platforms
4. Expand the best one into the weekly email
5. CTA every time: "the full story is in my weekly note → site"
6. The email nurtures; some readers become customers / sharers
```
One unit of real work → one video → one email → three platforms. That's the whole
machine, and it runs from your phone.

### Platform priorities for your situation
1. **TikTok + IG Reels (do these first).** Highest reach-per-effort for a new,
   unknown creator. Same vertical video posts to both. This is your discovery.
2. **YouTube (Shorts now, long-form when the podcast starts).** Slower to compound
   but the most durable and the most credible. Shorts can be the same clips.
3. **LinkedIn (underrated for you).** You sell B2B (law firms). Text posts about
   running a marketing firm reach exactly your customers, with almost no one your
   age competing there. High-leverage, low-effort, no video required.
4. **X/Threads (optional).** Only if you enjoy it. Don't spread yourself thin.

### What actually drives non-paid growth in 2026 (use these)
- **Hook discipline.** The first 2 seconds decide everything. "I lost a client this
  week and here's the mistake" beats "Here are 5 tips."
- **Volume + consistency over polish.** 5 rough videos/week beats 1 perfect one.
  The phone camera is fine. Done is the standard.
- **Series, not one-offs.** "Building to law school, week 12." Recurring formats
  train the algorithm and the audience.
- **Reply to comments with videos.** Free, compounding reach.
- **Borrow audiences.** Be a guest on small podcasts in marketing / founder /
  student-entrepreneur niches. You give value, they give you their audience, $0.
- **The newsletter recommendation network.** (See Part 6 — Beehiiv's built-in
  recommendations let other newsletters send you free subscribers.)
- **Teach your actual skill.** You run a marketing firm — make content that teaches
  small businesses one marketing thing. It's useful, shareable, and it markets _you_
  as the operator at the same time. Highest-trust content you can make.

### Realistic solo cadence (don't overcommit and quit)
- **3–5 short videos / week** (batch-film 2 sittings, post daily).
- **1 weekly email** (expand your best clip — you've already done the thinking).
- **2–3 LinkedIn text posts / week** (repurpose the same lessons).
- **Essays: 1–2 / month**, when you have something worth 800 words.

Consistency for 90 days beats intensity for 2 weeks. Build the smallest schedule
you can actually keep, then expand.

## Part 6 — The newsletter: a $0 stack and how to set it up

### Urgent fix first
Your site already has the code to talk to a newsletter provider (Beehiiv), but
**no provider is connected**, so in production the form silently saves emails to a
temporary location that gets erased. **Do not promote the form until a provider is
connected** — until then you're collecting signups into a void.

### Recommendation: **Beehiiv (free "Launch" plan)**
It's the right call for you specifically, because **the integration is already
built into your site** — you just add two keys and it works. On top of that:

- **Free up to 2,500 subscribers, unlimited sends.** You won't hit that for a while.
- **Custom domain + the signup form stays on _your_ site** (you own the experience).
- **A built-in recommendation network** — other newsletters can recommend yours and
  send you subscribers for free. This is real organic growth baked into the free tier.
- Trade-offs on free: Beehiiv branding in the footer, basic analytics, no
  automations. None of that matters at your stage.

### Free alternatives (for context)

| Platform | Free limit | Best for | Catch |
|---------|-----------|----------|-------|
| **Beehiiv (Launch)** | 2,500 subs, unlimited sends | **You** — already integrated, growth network | Beehiiv branding, no automations on free |
| **Kit (ConvertKit)** | 10,000 subs | Most generous limit, creator tooling | Would replace your built-in form/integration |
| **Substack** | Unlimited free subs | Built-in social discovery (Notes/recs) | You don't own the site experience; it's their platform |
| **MailerLite** | ~500–1,000 subs, 12k emails/mo | Polished automations | Lowest free subscriber cap |
| **EmailOctopus** | 2,500 subs, 10k emails/mo | Simple + cheap to scale | Fewer growth features |
| **Buttondown** | 100 subs | Minimalist, writer-first | Tiny free cap |

**Bottom line:** start on **Beehiiv free** because it's already wired in and has a
growth network. If you ever want a bigger free ceiling and don't mind swapping the
integration, **Kit** is the generous runner-up.

### Setup checklist (≈30 minutes, one time)
1. Create a free Beehiiv account and a publication.
2. In Beehiiv, grab your **API key** and **Publication ID**.
3. In your Vercel project settings, add two environment variables:
   `BEEHIIV_API_KEY` and `BEEHIIV_PUBLICATION_ID`. (Your site swaps from the stub to
   live automatically — no code change needed.)
4. Redeploy. Send yourself a test signup from the live form and confirm it lands in
   Beehiiv.
5. Turn on Beehiiv's **welcome email** so new subscribers immediately get something.
6. Turn on **Recommendations** in Beehiiv to start the free subscriber-swap network.
7. _(Optional, recommended)_ Create one **lead magnet** so there's a reason to
   subscribe: e.g. "My reading list" (you already have the Books page) or "The 5
   marketing moves every small law firm should make." Make it once; offer it forever.

### Make the first email exist
Write **issue #1 this week**, even if it's 200 words: who you are, what you're
building, what this newsletter will be, and one honest lesson from the week. A list
of 10 real people who hear from you beats a list of 1,000 who never do.

## Appendix — measure this, ignore that

**Track (in order):**
1. Email subscribers (the only owned number).
2. Email open rate (are you sending things people want?).
3. Which platform / video drives the most signups.

**Mostly ignore:** raw follower counts, individual pageviews, going viral once.
Vanity metrics feel good and pay nothing. A small, engaged email list that trusts
you is worth more than a big number that doesn't convert.

**The one sentence to remember:** _create on the platforms, capture on the site,
and keep the promise you make to the people who give you their email._
